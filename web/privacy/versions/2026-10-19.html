1. Core Operating Principles

This Privacy Policy explains how Counter (dev.jskw.count) ("the App", "we", "us", or "our"), authored by John Steel, handles information across our Android application (built with Tauri) and web synchronization services hosted at counter.jskw.dev.

  • Local by Default: Unless you choose to sync a counter, all counter counts and states remain stored strictly on your local device.
  • No User Accounts: You do not create an account, configure a password, or submit personal identity information (such as your name, phone number, or address) to use the app or sync counters.
  • Zero-Identity Synchronization: When you choose to sync a counter to the server, the server processes only the counter tally and its cryptographic identifiers. We do not know who you are.

2. Cloud Sync and Hashed Key Architecture

When you enable cloud synchronization for a counter, the App connects with our synchronization server using a dual-token cryptographic scheme:

  • Dual-UUID Generation: The server generates two distinct, random UUIDs when a counter is prepared for synchronization:
    • Public UUID (Read-Only): Can be shared freely. Anyone with access to this public UUID can view the numeric count on the web or in an app.
    • Private UUID (Read, Write & Delete): Serves as the authorization key and cipher. Possessing this key grants permission to increment, decrement, reset, modify, or permanently delete the counter. This key can be transferred between devices via QR code or manual input.
  • Hashed Storage Only (Zero Server Recovery):
    • The server does not persist raw UUIDs. Once generated and sent to your client, the raw UUIDs are discarded from active server memory.
    • The database stores only a one-way cryptographic hash of each UUID. When a client issues a request to read, write, or delete a counter, the incoming UUID is hashed on arrival and checked for an exact match against the stored hash.
    • UUIDs cannot be recovered: Because the database stores only one-way cryptographic hashes, lost Private UUIDs cannot be retrieved, reset, restored, or reconstructed by John Steel or the sync server under any circumstances.
  • Stored Records: Beyond the one-way cryptographic hashes, the server persists only the numeric counter tally and basic synchronization timestamps. Records are not tied to personal identities, IP addresses, or hardware fingerprints.

3. Key Security & User Responsibility

Notice on Key Responsibility: Because authorization is verified strictly through one-way cryptographic hashes, anyone holding the Public UUID can view your count, and anyone holding your Private UUID (or its QR code) can overwrite or permanently delete your count.

You are solely responsible for safeguarding your Private UUIDs and exported QR codes. If you share your Private UUID, you grant full write control over that counter. If you lose your Private UUID without a backup, write access is permanently lost.

4. Evaluation Telemetry and Tester-Only Opt-In

  • Minimum Evaluation Baseline: We collect only the absolute minimum non-identifying technical metadata (e.g., app version string, general OS level) strictly needed to decide whether future telemetry or diagnostic infrastructure is ever required.
  • Client Version Header & API Migration Metrics: Counter clients send a client version header (such as a User-Agent or X-Client-Version indicating the application build, e.g., android-<versionCode> or web-<git-hash>). This header is recorded solely in low-cardinality, non-identifying aggregate metrics and is only used to decide when we can safely deprecate legacy formats and update our REST API. It is never linked to individual counter IDs, IP addresses, or user identities.
  • Email-Gated Opt-In: Diagnostic logging and usage tracking cannot be enabled passively. It requires explicit, affirmative opt-in with an email address.
  • Tester Recommendation: We strongly recommend that nobody opt in to tracking unless they are a trusted tester and John Steel has asked them to opt in in person. Unless John Steel has personally spoken with you in person and requested test logs, please leave telemetry and tracking disabled.
  • Diagnostic Log Lifespan: Any opt-in diagnostic logs submitted by designated testers are automatically scheduled for hard deletion after 30 days and are never shared or combined with third-party datasets.

5. Data Retention, Deletion, and Server Removal

  • Local Data Deletion: Deleting a counter inside the local app immediately removes the local record and stored UUIDs from device storage.
  • Server Permanent Removal: You can permanently delete a synchronized counter from the server at any time by issuing a delete request with that counter's Private UUID from the App or web interface. Upon verifying the hash, the counter tally and both hashes are permanently erased from our database.
  • Inactivity Purge Policy: Unless explicitly told otherwise by the developer, all counters are purged from the server after 90 days of inactivity (measured as 90 consecutive days without any synchronization, increment, decrement, or access). All residual records, tombstone entries, and cryptographic hashes of purged counters will be permanently cleared from the server 30 days after that (a total of 120 days of inactivity).
  • Early Log Purge & GDPR Data Requests: You may request an early purge of your logs and counters at any time by emailing your Private (admin) token to counter@jskw.dev with GDPR REQUEST at the start of the subject line. We will purge all logs and counters associated with the provided token. Upon request, we will also optionally email you a ZIP archive containing all relevant logs with IP addresses redacted. We make every effort to respond as quickly as possible, though processing may take up to 30 days.
  • Self-Service Sync Verification: Users may inspect their public counter data at any time directly through the REST API or web interface without authenticating.
  • No Data Monetization: We do not sell, rent, monetize, or transfer your counter data, hashes, or diagnostic emails to data brokers, ad networks, or third parties.

6. Device Permissions

The Android application requests only the permissions strictly necessary for its features:

  • Internet / Network Access: Used solely to communicate with counter.jskw.dev when cloud sync is explicitly used.
  • Camera Permission (Optional): Used solely to scan a Private UUID QR code from another device. Camera frames are processed strictly in volatile memory on your device in real time and are never recorded, saved, or transmitted off-device.

7. Children's Privacy

Counter does not knowingly collect, solicit, or maintain personally identifiable information from children under the age of 13 (or under 16 where applicable by law).
